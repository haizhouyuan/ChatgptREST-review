# Website Design Research Brief

Date: 2026-04-27

This brief defines the design research needed before the next website implementation. The website project is now scoped as a pure Labebe DTC replacement site. AI demo, product matrix, Paperclip and boss-showroom materials are separate projects unless used only as presentation aids.

## Design Problem

The previous site iterations did not fail only because of color or layout. They failed because the website design was not sufficiently derived from a deep understanding of:

- the full Labebe product universe;
- which products are strongest or weakest;
- how shoppers enter the category;
- what Amazon VOC says about purchase anxieties;
- which products have enough visual material to carry a premium site;
- which website references map to which real Labebe product lines.

## Research Questions

1. What is Labebe after seeing all products: toy brand, children's furniture brand, playroom brand, gift brand, Montessori-inspired home brand, or multi-world family brand?
2. Which products deserve first-screen status and why?
3. Which shopping logic should dominate: age, room, occasion, product category, child development stage, problem/solution, or product world?
4. Which Amazon review themes should change PDP copy, FAQ, comparison modules and reassurance blocks?
5. Which product families should be shown as collections, bundles, room sets or editorial stories?
6. How should desktop use the width and scale better than the failed narrow-centered prototypes?
7. How should mobile make the shopping path fast and credible?
8. What should be deliberately excluded because it looks unsupported, fake, generic, or too AI-demo-like?

## Reference Categories To Revisit

Same-category and adjacent DTC:

- Lovevery: age/stage clarity and learning logic.
- Lalo: adult-taste child product DTC.
- Milton & Goose: premium wooden pretend-play world-building.
- Little Partners: learning tower and parent utility clarity.
- Crate & Kids / Pottery Barn Kids: room merchandising and registry/gift logic.
- Nestig: nursery/room journey and visual softness.
- Tender Leaf: wooden toy world charm.

World-class web references:

- Linear/Raycast: system clarity and interaction confidence, not for consumer visual style.
- Stripe Atlas/Framer: polished explanation, motion discipline, page rhythm.
- High-end editorial retail references: to be researched again after product intelligence, especially for image-led merchandising and mobile PDP.

## Required Design Options

Produce at least four distinct options. They cannot be the same layout with different colors.

### Option A: Growth-Space Home Brand

Labebe as a children's growth-space brand. Products are organized by room, routine and developmental stage. Best if product intelligence shows furniture/storage/learning tower strength.

Required pages:

- homepage with room/routine navigation
- room collection
- PDP with fit, dimensions, age and setup clarity
- mobile room finder

### Option B: Gift and Milestone Commerce

Labebe as a highly giftable child-milestone brand. Products are organized by first birthday, baby shower, grandparent gift, holidays and age bands. Best if rockers/push walkers win demand and visual distinctiveness.

Required pages:

- gift finder homepage path
- occasion collection
- PDP with gift reassurance, shipping, age, reviews and unboxing/media
- mobile gift quiz path

### Option C: Room Builder / Playroom Reset

Labebe as the brand that turns messy playrooms into calm, useful child spaces. Best if storage/shelf/desk products have enough evidence and visuals.

Required pages:

- before/after product story
- room-builder collection
- storage/furniture PDP
- bundle/cart path

### Option D: Pretend Play Editorial Worlds

Labebe as a maker of child-sized pretend worlds: kitchen, shop, bakery, laundry, garden, art corner. Best if play kitchen and pretend-play SKUs can visually carry the site.

Required pages:

- editorial homepage
- pretend-play world landing page
- PDP with story, use scenes and accessories
- video/story module

### Option E: Product Theater

A more daring, high-impact DTC homepage that uses product-scale, scroll choreography and scene transitions. It must still be a consumer shopping site, not an AI demo.

Required pages:

- full-viewport product-led opening with real products
- scroll transition between product worlds
- PDP with strong media theater
- mobile-first simplified version

## Required Output Per Option

For every option, produce:

- one-sentence positioning
- target shopper intent
- visual mood
- homepage structure
- collection structure
- PDP structure
- mobile path
- hero SKU candidates
- conversion mechanism
- required assets
- risks and why the option might fail
- examples to borrow from and what not to copy

## Presentation Format

The final presentation should include both static and motion:

1. Strategy board: why this direction exists.
2. Desktop and mobile key screens.
3. Click-through prototype or mocked route.
4. 60-90 second video walkthrough:
   - entry intent
   - navigation
   - PDP
   - add-to-cart or bundle
   - visual/interaction highlights
5. Appendix with product evidence and reference mapping.

## Design Acceptance Bar

A design option is not acceptable if:

- it could belong to any children/toy brand;
- it hides product facts behind atmosphere;
- it cannot explain why the hero products were chosen;
- it invents claims;
- it mixes internal AI demo modules into the consumer site;
- it looks weak at desktop scale;
- it lacks a credible mobile purchase path.
