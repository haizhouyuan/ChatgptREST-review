# Website Design Research: What the Labebe Site Must Become

## 1. Direct Diagnosis

The current Labebe prototype is materially better than the early Kimi draft. It fixed route stubs, fake reviews, product-data drift, non-Labebe placeholders, and the worst credibility failures. But the user reaction, "it still feels similar," is technically correct.

The reason is simple:

- The consumer side still reads as a warm DTC ecommerce template with product cards.
- The AI side still reads as a separate dashboard, not a transformation inside the shopping experience.
- The homepage does not yet create a single unforgettable interaction.
- The design library explains the method, but it does not feel like an executive-grade design artifact.
- The AI modules are mostly cards and labels. They do not visibly unfold a product into a growth system.

The next version must not only become prettier. It must change the site grammar.

## 2. North-Star Design Concept

Name:

> Labebe Growth-Space Engine

Definition:

> A real children's product page becomes an AI-assisted shopping, storytelling, merchandising, and growth workflow.

The first 30 seconds should feel like:

1. I see real Labebe products as a family, not a generic kids room.
2. I choose a parent scenario, such as "18-36m + nursery + first birthday".
3. The site recommends specific SKUs with source labels.
4. One SKU expands into a PDP story, a room/gift set, a TikTok storyboard, an Amazon A+ hero, a Google Shopping image prompt, an email block, and a claim-review state.
5. The site makes it obvious what is real data, what is AI draft, and what needs human review.

## 3. Design Excellence Framework

### 3.1 Brand Identity Layer

Labebe should not be framed as a generic wooden toy seller. It should be framed as a children's growth-space brand:

- plush rockers for first brave rides and milestone gifts;
- learning towers for kitchen participation and early independence;
- play kitchens and shops for pretend-play worlds;
- shelves and organizers for calm playroom routines;
- activity toys for screen-free focused play.

The visual system should combine:

- warm premium Montessori home;
- giftable childhood moments;
- tangible wood, plush, room, and routine cues;
- restrained AI overlays only where they explain an action.

### 3.2 Product Family Layer

The hero must show the product family. A single atmospheric room is not enough.

Recommended first-viewport composition:

- center: Pink Unicorn Plush Rocker product cutout or lifestyle image;
- left: Foldable Learning Tower and Cream Wooden Play Kitchen;
- right: Montessori Shelf and Toy Storage Organizer;
- foreground: "AI Gift Finder" decision chips;
- background: warm room scene with visible product silhouettes;
- side rail: "Product Truth", "Scene Draft", "Claim Review", "Channel Assets".

This makes the first viewport say: "Labebe has product breadth and AI can operate on it."

### 3.3 Guided Decision Layer

The strongest children-product sites are not only category catalogs. They help parents decide by age, stage, room, occasion, and task.

Lovevery is the strongest reference for age/stage discovery; Labebe should borrow the staged discovery pattern, not the subscription model or unsupported expert authority. Lalo is useful for adult-taste baby/toddler aesthetics. Milton and Goose is useful for premium wooden pretend-play storytelling. Crate & Kids, Pottery Barn Kids, and Nestig are useful for room and gift merchandising. Little Partners is useful for learning-tower participation framing.

Labebe's navigation should expose:

- Shop by Age;
- Shop by Room;
- Shop by Occasion;
- Shop by Play Goal;
- Gift Finder;
- Room Builder;
- AI Growth Studio, as a separate demo route.

### 3.4 Product Story Layer

Every priority SKU needs a reusable story spine:

| SKU | Story Spine | PDP Module | Channel Asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pink Unicorn Plush Rocker | first birthday, nursery corner, first brave ride | gift-first PDP intro, milestone storyboard | TikTok gift hook, email gift guide |
| Foldable Learning Tower | kitchen participation, parent-child routine, independence | routine scenes, fit/dimensions, safety questions | VOC-to-concept, Google lifestyle image |
| Cream Wooden Play Kitchen | tiny chef, pretend restaurant, role play | play scenario sequence | 15s pretend-play video storyboard |
| Montessori Shelf | toy rotation, child-accessible order, calm playroom | before/after, shelf zones | Pinterest room reset, SEO guide |
| Storage Organizer | parent relief, small-space cleanup | room fit and bundle | playroom reset landing page |

### 3.5 Interaction Layer

Motion should show transformation, not decoration.

High-value motions:

- Finder chips collapse into recommended SKU cards.
- A SKU card expands into a radial channel asset wall.
- Product facts highlight the data source used by each generated asset.
- A risky AI claim gets blocked and rewritten in a claim gate.
- A room set assembles from product cutouts with a bundle price.
- A PDP story mode flips between "Shopper View" and "Growth View".

Avoid:

- generic AI orbs;
- unrelated 3D objects;
- galaxy/cyber dashboards;
- animated numbers pretending to be real ROI;
- fake autonomous personalization.

### 3.6 Trust and Data Layer

The "truth layer" should become visible design, not only documentation.

Recommended UI primitives:

- Source pill: Scraped / Official / To be confirmed.
- Claim state: Draft / Needs review / Review-ready / Blocked.
- Product fact drawer: price, review count, image source, unknown fields.
- AI output footer: "Generated from product data + channel template. Safety/material claims require manual approval."

This is more impressive than hiding governance, because it proves AI can be controlled.

## 4. Benchmark Interpretation

| Lalo | adult-taste child-product styling | over-minimal layouts that hide SKU breadth | clean product family hero and bundle sets |

| Nestig | nursery environment and gentle room storytelling | limited category breadth | nursery set builder and softer PDP story |

## 5. What a Better Homepage Should Do

### First Viewport

Layout:

- full-bleed but controlled product-family scene;
- real product cards embedded into the scene, not isolated below it;
- right side has an "AI Growth Lens" mini-panel showing states:
  - Product Truth;
  - Finder Result;
  - Scene Draft;
  - Claim Gate;
  - Channel Pack.

Primary action:

- "Find a Gift" routes to an interactive finder.

Secondary action:

- "Show AI Growth Demo" triggers a SKU expansion overlay, not just navigation.

### Scroll Section 1: Guided Choice

Interactive:

- choose age, room, occasion, parent goal;
- see three recommended SKUs;
- each SKU card includes a reason and source state.

### Scroll Section 2: Product Story Wall

Instead of generic best sellers:

- four story lanes: Giftable Rockers, Kitchen Helpers, Pretend Play Worlds, Playroom Reset;
- each lane has a product, a parent scenario, a PDP module, and one channel asset preview.

### Scroll Section 3: One SKU Explosion

Use Pink Unicorn Plush Rocker:

- center SKU card;
- fan-out cards for PDP, TikTok, Amazon A+, Google image prompt, email gift guide, Pinterest pin, claim gate;
- status labels and generated preview snippets;
- a "show source" toggle.

This should be the memorable demo moment.

## 6. Design Artifacts to Build

The site should include a real design library, not just a documentation route:

- visual direction board;
- benchmark cards with "borrow / avoid / Labebe mapping";
- token table;
- component states;
- motion rules;
- data truth overlay examples;
- claim gate examples;
- forbidden patterns;
- boss demo route map.

The Design Library should look like a serious creative system, not a markdown dump converted to cards.

## 7. High-Bar Principles

1. The product must be visible in the first viewport.
2. AI must visibly transform a SKU, not merely annotate it.
3. Every impressive claim must show source status.
4. The buying path must remain intact.
5. The internal AI Studio must be dark, precise, and Labebe-specific.
6. Mobile must be designed, not just scaled down.
7. Screenshots and demo paths matter as much as code.
8. The site should show taste, judgment, and operational control, not only generation.
